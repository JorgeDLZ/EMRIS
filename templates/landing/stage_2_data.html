Data engineering

How the raw files become the clean, linked and documented data the models are trained on.

Selected sources and features

The raw files are kept exactly as downloaded in data/; the loader maps their Spanish columns and labels to the English schema. Of the 61 UNGRD columns EMRIS keeps the date, department, municipality, event type, the impact counts and the comment. Each component then uses only what it needs:

  • Impact estimator: event type, department, municipality and month — only fields known when the emergency is reported.
  • Emergency profiles: the nine impact variables (deaths, injuries, missing people, people affected, families affected, homes destroyed, homes damaged, roads damaged and hectares).
  • Similar emergencies: type, place, month and the impact variables.
  • Municipal risk: per municipality, the number of emergencies, the share of the three main types, the share of high impact, the mean impact and the shallow seismic activity from the SGC catalog.
Missing values, duplicates, inconsistencies and outliers
  • Discarded rows: 1 row with an unknown event type (unknown event type: 1). Rows with an invalid date are also discarded; the 2025 file has none. 6,505 of the 6,506 rows are kept.
  • Numbers: values written with thousands separators, such as “1,700”, are parsed; negative and non-numeric values would be set to 0 (the 2025 file has none).
  • Text: comments and SGC place names are checked for broken accents (mojibake) and repaired; this changes no UNGRD comment and 14 SGC place names, such as “GuapotÃ¡” to “Guapotá”.
  • Places: department and municipality names are matched to DIVIPOLA after removing accents, with 25 explicit aliases for names written differently, such as “CALI”. The 24 rows reported at department level are kept with no municipality.
  • Duplicates: no row is fully duplicated. One emergency code is repeated on two department-level floods with different dates; both are kept. The load commands can run again without creating duplicates.
  • Outliers: very large values (up to 44,476 people affected or 60,000 hectares, against medians of 20 and 3) are kept, because they are the high-impact cases. Profiles and similarity use log1p scaling so they do not dominate the distances.
Encoding, scaling and feature engineering
  • Categories: event type, department and municipality are one-hot encoded. In the impact estimator, categories seen fewer than 3 times are grouped, and a category never seen in training is ignored instead of breaking the prediction.
  • Month: encoded as its sine and cosine, so December and January are close.
  • Impact variables: log1p followed by standardisation in profiles and similarity.
  • Municipal features: standardised before clustering.
  • Engineered features: the target is_high_impact, the response actions extracted from the comments by keyword rules (6,436 emergencies have at least one action) and, per municipality, the count and maximum magnitude of shallow earthquakes (depth below 70 km, magnitude 4.0 and above) within 50 km of its centre.

Transformations and their justification
  • Report-time features only for the impact estimator: at report time the impact is not known yet, so using it would leak the answer.
  • Grouping rare categories and ignoring unseen ones: new municipalities and rare event types appear in operation and must not break a prediction.
  • log1p on impact counts: the counts are heavily skewed, and without it a few large emergencies would decide every cluster and every neighbor.
  • Cyclic month: seasonality (dry season wildfires, rainy season floods and landslides) is circular.
  • Type-wise 90th percentile in the target: what counts as large differs by event type; 1,700 hectares is large for a wildfire, while a flood is measured in people affected.
Training, validation and test partitions

The split is by time, so the models are always tested on emergencies that happened after the ones they learned from:

  • Fit: January–August (4,881 emergencies).
  • Validation: September (631) — used to choose between the candidate models and to tune the decision threshold.
  • Test: October–December (993, of which 193 high impact) — used only to report the final metrics, scored by models refitted on January–September.
  • Cross-validation: five expanding folds, each validating on one month from May to September and fitting on all earlier months.

The encoders are part of a scikit-learn Pipeline, so they are fitted on the training rows only in every split. After the evaluation, the stored model is refitted on all rows.
